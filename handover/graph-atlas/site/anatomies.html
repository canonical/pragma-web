05 / 16 · the anatomy census

181 written anatomies, 882 nodes, one grammar

Every block carries an ds:anatomyDsl property, but only 181 of the 325 hold a document yet; 147 are empty strings and 5 do not parse. This page reads the 176 that do: what the grammar looks like, who has an anatomy and who has not, every tree drawn small, and which blocks are built out of which.

The grammar, on the Button

The DSL is YAML. One node is the block; edges nest further nodes; a relation on an edge names the slot a consumer fills and how many children may appear; styles bind a style key, optionally per state, to a token or a literal. Counts on the right are across all 176 parsed documents.


  


  

    
Who has an anatomy
Written and parsed (blue), written but malformed (orange), still empty (gray), by tier and by grain. The global tier is the best covered; Launchpad and Landscape, the two large product tiers, have written almost none.

    

    

  


  

    
Every anatomy, drawn small
All 176 parsed trees as glyphs, largest first. Filled discs are named nodes (they point at another block); hollow discs are anonymous roles; the square is the root. Click a glyph for the full outline with slots, cardinalities and style keys.

    

  


  

    
Which blocks are built out of which
A named node inside an anatomy is a reference to another block. Following those references across all documents gives the composition graph: an arrow runs from the containing block to the contained one. Node size is how many anatomies contain the block. Blocks referenced but without an anatomy of their own are drawn hollow.
